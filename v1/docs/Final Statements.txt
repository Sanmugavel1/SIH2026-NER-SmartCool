Design of an Offline-First Edge-IoT System for Cold-Chain Risk Detection and Equipment-Failure Prediction
Problem statement

Design an embedded cold-chain monitoring system that detects and predicts unsafe storage conditions for vaccines and temperature-sensitive medicines during storage and last-mile transportation, particularly in locations with unreliable electricity and network connectivity.

Issue

Conventional temperature loggers generally record temperature excursions after they occur. A more useful system should identify factors that may cause an excursion, including:

Power failure
Refrigerator door left open
Increasing internal temperature
Excessive compressor cycling
Low backup-battery condition
Improper placement of medical products
Delay during transportation

India’s eVIN framework already uses automated temperature loggers and mobile alerts. Therefore, the project should not merely duplicate temperature monitoring. It should focus on offline operation, predictive alerts, last-mile transport, power-failure resilience or low-cost deployment in smaller facilities.

Significance

Temperature monitoring is essential for maintaining appropriate vaccine-storage conditions. WHO guidance also recognises the need to prepare for equipment failure, power outages and temperature excursions during storage and transportation.

Innovative concept

Build a device using temperature probes, humidity, door-state sensing, mains-power detection and a thermal prediction model. The device can estimate the remaining safe-storage period and send alerts through GSM, LoRa or Bluetooth when internet connectivity is unavailable.

Potential impact
Reduced vaccine and medicine spoilage
Faster response to refrigerator failures
Reliable records for quality audits
Improved last-mile healthcare delivery
Reduced financial loss in primary health centres
Scalable monitoring for blood banks and diagnostic laboratories

Offline-First Cold-Chain Integrity Monitoring
Recommended project title

Design of an Offline-First Edge-IoT System for Cold-Chain Risk Detection and Equipment-Failure Prediction

Why it ranks first

This is the easiest project to complete reliably because the monitored variables—temperature, humidity, door status and power availability—can be reproduced and tested safely in a laboratory. It is derived directly from SIH MA1216, which calls for monitoring risks affecting temperature-sensitive products and generating timely alerts.

Minimum viable prototype

The device should:

Continuously monitor refrigerator and product-zone temperature.
Detect prolonged door opening and power failure.
Store readings locally when the internet is unavailable.
Produce buzzer, display and mobile alerts.
Synchronise stored records when connectivity returns.
Estimate whether temperature is rising toward an unsafe condition.
Suggested hardware
Component	Purpose
ESP32	Main controller and Wi-Fi/BLE communication
Two DS18B20 probes	Air and product-simulation temperature
SHT31/DHT22	Humidity measurement
Magnetic reed switch	Refrigerator-door monitoring
DS3231 RTC	Accurate timestamping during network failure
MicroSD module	Offline data storage
Buzzer and OLED	Local warning and status
Battery/UPS module	Operation during power failure
Optional GSM/LoRa	Remote alerts without Wi-Fi

Planning budget: ₹4,000–₹7,000, excluding the refrigerator and laptop.

Ten-week action plan
Weeks	Activity
1	Define temperature limits, system architecture and test conditions
2–3	Connect and calibrate sensors; implement local display and alarms
4	Add RTC-based logging and microSD storage
5	Develop Flask or MQTT dashboard
6	Implement store-and-forward offline synchronisation
7	Add temperature-rise prediction and alert prioritisation
8	Conduct door-open, power-cut and communication-loss tests
9	Run continuous 48–72-hour validation
10	Final enclosure, documentation and demonstration
Evaluation metrics
Temperature-measurement error
Alert-generation delay
Battery-backup duration
Percentage of records recovered after reconnection
Number of false alarms
Prediction time before a threshold excursion
Societal impact

The system could support small pharmacies, vaccine transport boxes, rural clinics, laboratories, dairy sellers and food-storage operators. The prototype should be positioned as an early-warning and record-keeping system, not as a certified medical device.

Low-Cost Livestock Health and Behaviour Monitoring for Small Farmers
Problem statement

Develop an affordable wearable collar or ear-tag system that detects abnormal activity, feeding behaviour, body temperature and possible illness in cattle or goats and sends simple alerts to farmers.

Issue

Small farmers may identify illness, heat stress, reduced feeding or reproductive changes only after visible symptoms appear. Existing commercial livestock-wearable systems can be too expensive for farms with only a few animals.

Significance

FAO identifies animal health, livestock productivity, emergency response and smallholder resilience as important objectives for sustainable livestock development.

Innovative concept

Use an accelerometer, temperature sensor and microphone to estimate activity, resting time, rumination and abnormal behaviour. A LoRa gateway can serve an entire village, while alerts can be delivered through SMS or a Tamil mobile interface.

Potential impact
Earlier illness detection
Reduced animal mortality
Improved reproductive management
Lower veterinary intervention delay
Better productivity for small farmers
Creation of locally relevant livestock datasets

Low-Cost Livestock Health and Behaviour Monitoring
Recommended project title

Offline LoRa-Based Livestock Behaviour and Abnormality Alert System for Small Farmers

Why it ranks third

This project offers the strongest direct benefit to rural communities and has entrepreneurship potential. The hardware is manageable, but collecting reliable animal-behaviour data requires more time than cold-chain or machine monitoring. It should therefore be selected only when access to cattle, goats or a farm is available.

Minimum viable prototype

A collar-mounted unit should monitor:

General movement and inactivity
Standing, lying and walking patterns
Possible restlessness
Collar-zone temperature
Prolonged reduction in activity
Communication loss and battery status

The first prototype should provide abnormal-behaviour alerts, not attempt to diagnose a specific disease.

Suggested hardware
Component	Purpose
ESP32-C3 or nRF52	Low-power embedded controller
MPU6050/ICM-20948	Movement and posture sensing
Digital temperature sensor	Collar-zone temperature trend
LoRa module	Long-range, low-power communication
RTC	Time-based activity logging
Rechargeable battery	Wearable power source
Waterproof enclosure and collar	Field deployment
Raspberry Pi/ESP32 gateway	Data reception and dashboard
Optional microphone	Rumination or vocalisation research

Planning budget: ₹5,000–₹10,000 for one collar and gateway.

Fourteen-week action plan
Weeks	Activity
1	Select animal type and define only observable behaviours
2–3	Assemble low-power collar prototype
4	Develop accelerometer and temperature logging
5	Implement LoRa communication and local storage
6	Optimise sleep modes and battery consumption
7–8	Collect labelled motion data through controlled movement tests
9–10	Conduct limited supervised trials on one or more animals
11	Extract activity level, posture and inactivity-duration features
12	Implement rule-based or TinyML abnormality detection
13	Measure false alerts, range and battery endurance
14	Improve enclosure, dashboard and documentation
Evaluation metrics
Activity-classification accuracy
False abnormality-alert rate
Battery life
LoRa communication range
Packet-loss rate
Wearability and enclosure stability
Time required to identify prolonged inactivity
Societal impact

The project could help small farmers observe animals remotely, particularly during night hours or when animals graze away from the house. Its realistic benefit is earlier attention to unusual behaviour and reduced manual monitoring effort. Increased farmer income is a possible long-term outcome, but it should not be claimed without field evidence.

Main feasibility condition

Farmer cooperation, ethical handling and labelled field data are essential. Without farm access, this project becomes difficult to validate convincingly.
